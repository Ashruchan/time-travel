Foundations

What is Time?

Time is the continuous progression of events from the past, through the present, and into the future. We usually experience time as a line, but in modern physics it is tightly linked with space and gravity.

Foundations

What is Time Travel?

Time travel is the idea of moving between different moments in time, analogous to motion in space. Einstein’s work showed that the laws of physics do not entirely exclude such possibilities.

Physics

Einstein and relativity

According to the theory of relativity, time and space form a single fabric. Clocks run slower at very high speeds or in strong gravitational fields.

Einstein described time like a river: near massive objects such as black holes, its flow slows measurably.

Physics

Time dilation

A well-known illustration appears in Interstellar: near the black hole Gargantua, gravitational time dilation becomes extreme.

On Miller’s planet, about one hour corresponded to seven years on Earth — a dramatic but physically grounded idea.

Observation

A real-world example

Astronaut Scott Kelly spent roughly a year on the International Space Station. Relativity and orbital motion mean he aged very slightly less than his twin on Earth — by milliseconds, but measurably.

Speculation

Dimensions and higher beings

We navigate three spatial dimensions; time appears to move in one direction for us.

A hypothetical four-dimensional observer might perceive past and future together and move through time in ways we cannot.

Theory

Wormholes

Wormholes, or Einstein–Rosen bridges, are hypothetical tunnels linking distant regions of spacetime. They are not observed in nature today but remain an active theoretical topic.

Culture

Mythology and time travel

In the Vishnu Purana, King Kakudmi visits Brahma Lok with his daughter Revati; when they return, many ages have passed on Earth — a narrative sometimes compared with time dilation.

Limits

Paradoxes and constraints

The grandfather paradox illustrates how backward time travel can clash with consistent causality. Changing the past so that the traveler never exists is logically troublesome.

Conservation laws and thermodynamics also suggest that altering history is far from a free rewrite of events.

Reflection

A philosophical view

“We are all time travelers.”

Some people live mentally in the past; others in an imagined future. The present is where experience actually occurs — and where choices matter.

Summary

Conclusion

Forward time travel at different rates is compatible with relativity and has tiny real analogues. Travel into the past faces serious obstacles from causality, thermodynamics, and the ordering of causes and effects.
